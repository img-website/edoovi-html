How LLMs reduce clinical documentation overhead by 40%

DG

Dr. Dev Gupta

Medical Director, Edoovi Labs · August 06, 2026

Clinical documentation has long been one of the primary drivers of clinician burnout globally. Modern research indicates that for every hour a physician spends with a patient, they spend up to two additional hours updating EHR logs, structuring pathology summaries, and coordinating discharge notes.

At Edoovi Labs, we integrated Large Language Models (LLMs) directly into the core workflows of the Edoovi Hospital Management System (HMS). The result? A clinical documentation pipeline that reduces time-to-chart by 40%, keeping doctors focused on what matters most: patient care.

HIPAA Compliancy Notice

All ambient documentation processing takes place in zero-retention API wrappers. No patient health information (PHI) is cached or utilized for external foundation model training.

1. Ambient Clinical Intelligence (ACI) & Automated Intake

Instead of manually typing long reports, doctors can utilize structured dictation models and conversational intake logs via Edoovi HMS. By using HIPAA-compliant speech-to-text models combined with fine-tuned LLMs, clinical conversations are automatically summarized into standardized medical templates:

  • Conversational Transcription: Natural dialogue between patient and doctor is captured and parsed.
  • Intelligent Summarization: The engine filters out non-clinical chat and structures the consultation notes under SOAP (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) guidelines.
  • Integration with WhatsApp API: Patients can update basic symptoms before arriving, which pre-fills the intake log.

2. Seamless discharge compilations & multi-hospital sync

Preparing discharge papers is often delayed by fragmented medical records. Edoovi HMS solves this by analyzing current lab logs, drug orders, and doctor daily checklists to generate a comprehensive discharge draft in one click.

"By integrating specialized LLMs in the ward management system, we observed a reduction of discharge delay times from 120 minutes down to just 35 minutes per patient."

By structuring information in a single sync database, administrative units can monitor capacity, billings, and compliance status in real-time.
